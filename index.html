1 · What happened?

A reviewer-conduct issue became a paper-level sanction.

The collective appeal does not dispute NeurIPS's authority to investigate or enforce confidentiality and unauthorized-LLM rules. It challenges a separate step: how the resulting desk-rejection sanctions were imposed on submitted papers.

01 · Notice

First notice at the end

The appeal states that affected authors first learned of the desk-rejection sanction in the final decision.

02 · Appeal

No pre-sanction appeal

The appeal states that no opportunity to appeal the proposed sanction was provided before it was imposed.

03 · Response

No prior reviewer response

The relevant reviewer-authors were not asked to respond before the sanction was communicated, according to the appeal.

04 · Authority

Board process not disclosed

The appeal does not claim that no Board action occurred; it asks NeurIPS to confirm whether the published Board process happened.

What this appeal is not arguing

It is not asking NeurIPS to excuse unauthorized LLM use or to abandon enforcement of reviewer rules.

It asks whether a reviewer-policy allegation and a separate paper-level sanction were connected through the procedure and decision-making authority established by NeurIPS's own published policies.

2 · Questions NeurIPS should be able to answer

The dispute turns on procedure, authority, and individualized sanctioning.

These are the core questions raised by the collective appeal. They can be answered independently of whether an underlying reviewer-policy violation occurred.

Where was the pre-sanction appeal?

The appeal cites the Academic Integrity Policy's statement that a Submitter or Reviewer will have an opportunity to appeal before the imposition of a sanction.

Who authorized the sanction?

The appeal asks whether Program Chairs recommended the sanction to the Board and whether the Board approved it by majority vote, or what published provision authorized a different process.

What kind of decision was this?

Were the Reject decisions Academic Integrity sanctions, ordinary scientific paper decisions, or actions under another published procedure?

Which policy controls?

The appeal asks how the FAQ's “no appeals” language is reconciled with the standing Academic Integrity Policy's pre-sanction appeal language.

Why this sanction in each case?

The appeal asks what specific conduct, severity assessment, relevant information, and individualized reasoning supported desk rejection for each affected submission.

Was desk rejection automatic?

The appeal notes that the 2026 AI-reviewing policy describes desk rejection as a possible consequence and asks for the published basis if every qualifying violation automatically triggered it.

Process described in the appeal

  1. Determine whether a violation occurred.
  2. Consider the appropriate sanction and relevant circumstances.
  3. Provide the applicable opportunity to appeal before sanction.
  4. Use the decision-making authority established by the governing policy.
→

Process the appellants ask NeurIPS to explain

  1. When was the sanction first proposed?
  2. When could the affected party respond or appeal?
  3. Who selected and approved the paper-level sanction?
  4. What individualized basis supported that sanction?
The central distinction

A conduct finding and a paper-level sanction are not the same decision.

The appeal argues that even if reviewer conduct violated a policy, that does not by itself establish that every resulting desk-rejection sanction was imposed through the required authority and procedure.

3 · What the appellants requested

Review the process, suspend the challenged sanctions, and restore papers where required safeguards were not followed.

The requested relief does not ask NeurIPS to directly accept any affected paper.

Suspend the challenged desk-rejection sanctions while the appeal is being resolved.

Identify whether the Reject decisions were Academic Integrity sanctions, ordinary paper decisions, or actions under another published procedure.

Identify the published procedure and decision-making authority governing those decisions.

Determine whether the required pre-sanction appeal opportunity and Board action occurred.

Determine whether each sanction reflected individualized consideration of nature, severity, and circumstances.

If the FAQ is treated as changing the Academic Integrity Policy, identify the applicable written Board amendment or rescission.

Vacate sanctions where the applicable procedural requirements were not satisfied.

Restore affected submissions to the scientific review and decision process that otherwise would have applied.
